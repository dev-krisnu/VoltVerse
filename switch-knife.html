Switch #1: Knife Switch (SPST)

Hinged copper blade manual isolation disconnector

Description & Definition

A Knife Switch is a type of switch used to control the flow of electricity in a circuit. It consists of a hinged copper blade that swings on a pivot to make contact with fixed spring jaws (clips). When the blade is inserted into the jaw, the circuit closes.

Core Working Principle

Operation is entirely manual. Pushing down on the insulated handle forces the conductive copper blade between friction contact jaws, completing the conductive path. Pulling the handle upward swings the blade out of contact, creating a visible air gap isolator break.

Voltage Range Up to 600V DC/AC
Current Rating 30A - 600A

Key Components

  • •
    Base Plate: Non-conductive Slate, Porcelain, or Bakelite board providing electrical insulation and physical mounting.
  • •
    Hinged Blade: High-conductivity hard-drawn copper bar attached to a pivot bracket.
  • •
    Contact Jaws: Spring-loaded brass or copper clips engineered to tightly grip the blade under friction.
  • •
    Insulated Handle: Rubber, wood, or plastic grip protecting the operator from electric shock.

Electrical Lab Application

In diploma electrical machines laboratories, DPST or DPDT knife switches are heavily used on DC motor starter panels for main supply isolation.

Arc Flash Safety Precaution:

Never open a knife switch under full load! Because it lacks arc-chutes, breaking live current will draw an exposed electric arc, which can burn contacts or injure the operator.
