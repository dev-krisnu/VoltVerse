Switch #7: Centrifugal Switch

Shaft-mounted speed-sensitive automatic starter winding disconnector

Operational Purpose

A Centrifugal Switch is an automatic, speed-sensitive electromechanical device mounted on the rotor shaft of single-phase induction motors (such as split-phase or capacitor-start motors). Its primary function is to keep the high-torque auxiliary (starting) winding energized at standstill and disconnect it once the rotor reaches approximately 75% to 80% of rated synchronous speed.

Centrifugal Force Physics Principle

The switch operates based on classical rotational dynamics:

Fc = m · ω2 · r

As motor shaft angular velocity (ω) increases, centrifugal force overcomes the holding spring tension, flinging flyweights outward and sliding the contact plate back to open electrical contacts.

Cut-off Threshold 75% - 80% Ns
Contact Configuration Normally Closed (At Rest)

Assembly & Structural Parts

  • •
    Rotating Governor Mechanism: Steel collar keyed to motor rotor shaft carrying hinged flyweights and calibrated tension springs.
  • •
    Spring-Loaded Flyweights: Calibrated metal counterweights that pivot radially outward under centrifugal acceleration.
  • •
    Sliding Contact Spool / Collar: Bakelite insulating cylinder sliding axially along the shaft to push or release contact arms.
  • •
    Stationary Contact Plate: Fixed housing mounted inside motor end-bell carrying silver-alloy contact points wired to auxiliary winding.

Laboratory Experiments & Fault Diagnosis

Capacitor-Start Motor Test Routine: Observe motor current spike during startup. Verify sharp "click" sound at ~75% speed indicating centrifugal switch opening and drop in line current.
Fault 1: Contacts Stuck CLOSED Starting winding and capacitor remain continuously energized, causing severe overheating, high humming noise, and winding burnout within minutes.
Fault 2: Contacts Stuck OPEN Motor fails to produce starting torque, hums loudly without rotating, and trips main line circuit breaker / thermal overload.
