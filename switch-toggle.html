Switch #2: Toggle Switch (Tumbler)

Snap-action lever mechanism for control & power distribution

Description & Definition

A Toggle Switch (or tumbler switch) is a manually operated mechanical switch actuated by a lever or bat handle that tilts back and forth to open or close an electric circuit. It uses an internal quick-make, quick-break spring mechanism to minimize contact arcing.

Snap-Action Detent Mechanism

As the toggle lever is pushed, an internal compression spring compresses against a plunger or detent ball until it passes the center pivot point. Once past center, the spring snaps the contacts open or closed rapidly, regardless of how slowly the handle is moved.

Voltage Range 12V DC - 250V AC
Current Rating 1A - 30A

Key Components

  • •
    Bat Handle / Actuator Lever: Metallic or plastic lever pivoted inside the central bushing.
  • •
    Threaded Bushing & Nut: Brass or chrome-plated collar allowing panel-mount installation.
  • •
    Bakelite Case: High-dielectric strength insulating body containing internal contacts.
  • •
    Movable Contact Arm: Silver-plated copper rocker driven by the internal plunger spring.

Diploma Practical Applications

Toggle switches are widely used across electrical engineering laboratories and industrial control consoles:

  • Main DC control supply switches on experimental test benches.
  • Function generator and oscilloscope power selection.
  • Two-way light control simulation circuits (using SPDT toggle switches).
  • Motor forward/reverse selector control circuits.
