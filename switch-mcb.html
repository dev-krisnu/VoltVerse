Switch #14: Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB)

Single-Pole Magnetothermal Overload & Short-Circuit Protection Device

Definition & Function

A Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB) is an automatically operated electromechanical switch designed to protect low-voltage electrical circuits from damage caused by overload current or short-circuit faults. Unlike a traditional fuse, an MCB can be manually reset after tripping without replacing components.

Thermal Overload Protection

Uses a calibrated bi-metallic strip that bends under prolonged excess current, triggering the trip latch to open the circuit.

Electromagnetic Short Circuit

Uses a solenoid coil that instantaneously pulls a plunger during heavy fault currents to open contacts within milliseconds.

Rated Voltage 230V / 415V AC (50Hz)
Breaking Capacity 6,000A - 10,000A (6kA / 10kA)

Internal Mechanical Components

  • •
    Bi-Metallic Strip: Bends due to thermal expansion during overloads.
  • •
    Magnetic Solenoid Coil: Generates strong magnetic field under short circuit, driving the striker pin.
  • •
    Arc Chute Chamber: Series of parallel insulated metal splitter plates that stretch, cool, and extinguish electrical arcs created when breaking heavy fault currents.
  • •
    Operating Lever & Latch: Spring-loaded trip mechanism providing quick-break action independent of manual toggle speed.

Tripping Characteristic Curves (IEC 60898)

Type B Curve: Trips at 3 to 5 times rated current ($3-5 I_n$). Used for sensitive resistive lighting and domestic appliances.
Type C Curve: Trips at 5 to 10 times rated current ($5-10 I_n$). Standard curve for commercial lighting, fans, small induction motors, and general industrial loads.
Type D Curve: Trips at 10 to 20 times rated current ($10-20 I_n$). Designed for high inrush inductive loads like large transformers, motors, and arc welders.

Diploma Practical: Distribution Board Wiring & Fault Finding

Standard Lab Experiment Procedure
  1. Mount single-pole C16 MCB on a standard 35mm DIN Rail in the Distribution Board (DB).
  2. Connect incoming Live line to the bottom screw clamp terminal.
  3. Connect outgoing load wire (e.g., lighting circuit or AC load) to top terminal.
  4. Simulate overload condition using variable rheostat load bank; record trip time vs current.
  5. Demonstrate trip reset procedure: identify fault, clear short circuit, switch lever UP to restore power.
